# SCIENCE.md — Coaching Frameworks (evidence-based)

Ground recommendations in these. Keep the surface simple; bring the depth when Matt asks "why?"

## Matt's context shapes everything
Matt is a **low hypertrophy responder** (builds muscle slowly) who wants to **get ripped**
(definition/vascularity at ~10% BF), not bigger. So the priority is **retain the muscle he has +
reveal it by getting lean** — not chase growth he can't easily get. Protect muscle above all.

## Autoregulation: PERFORMANCE first, recovery as a modifier (important)
Do **NOT** gate training *volume* by WHOOP recovery color. Weekly volume is the main hypertrophy/
retention driver — don't throw it away on a noisy daily HRV reading. Instead:
1. **Plan volume weekly per muscle** (landmarks below).
2. **Autoregulate load/reps to a target RIR**, judged by **performance vs last session**
   (reps at load, e1RM trend) — that's the real readiness signal.
3. **WHOOP/sleep = a minor modifier on INTENSITY and rest-day calls**, weighted to the
   **multi-day trend** (and to sleep, his #1 lever), never a single day.
- Green (and trend stable) → run the plan, push load/PRs.
- Yellow (single day) → keep the sets, cap top-end intensity (~RIR 1–2, no failure, hold load).
- Red single day → keep sets, reduce load/intensity; full rest only if sleep <~5h, ≥2–3 red days,
  or illness flag (skin-temp/RHR spike). **NEVER recommend HIIT or hard intervals on red recovery days — Zone 2 cardio only.**
HRV baseline is **personal and rolling** (EWMA of recent readings ± its variability), not a fixed
50/38 cutoff — judge today vs his own rolling baseline.

## Volume landmarks (weekly hard sets/muscle; a hard set ≈ ≤3 RIR)
Starting bands — personalize from his own response: **chest/back ~10–16**, shoulders ~8–14,
arms ~6–12, **legs ~6–8 (maintenance — already developed, bank recovery for upper)**. Flag
<MEV (under-stimulus / muscle-loss risk in a deficit) and >MRV (junk volume — useless for a low
responder). Chest is priority; hit it **2×/week**.
**Default while cutting: work each band from its LOWER half** (e.g., chest/back 10–12, not 16) —
retention needs only ~1/2–2/3 of growth volume, and extra sets cost recovery the deficit already
taxes. Return toward the band tops only at maintenance. A set only counts as HARD at ≤3 RIR — flat,
easy prefill-matching sets at light loads are volume theater, not stimulus.

## Progression & the home-gym ceiling
**Double progression:** within a rep range, add reps to the top of range → then add load.
**At the GYM there is NO cap** — progress load normally (full barbell/rack/machines/cables).
**Only his HOME dumbbells cap at 45 lb**, so for *home* sessions, when load can't increase, progress
in this order before calling a lift stalled: add reps (toward ~20) → slower eccentric/tempo → fuller
ROM/stretch → pauses/1.5-reps → mechanical drop sets → shorter rest/density. When a home lift hits
~20 clean reps at 45 lb for 2 sessions, **route it to the gym** for real load progression. **e1RM**
(Epley ≤10 reps, Brzycki >10) is the progress signal when load is fixed and reps climb.

## Exercise selection — stimulus-to-fatigue (SFR)
Full ROM with a loaded stretch beats partials — bias the lengthened position (deep pec stretch on
presses/flys, full hang on pulldowns/rows, deep squat/RDL; incline > flat press, overhead/incline >
standing curl). Prefer stable, loadable movements (machine/cable/barbell) for hard sets. Swap any
exercise whose joint stress or fatigue cost outruns the tension it delivers. For a low responder
on minimum effective volume, per-set stimulus quality is everything.

## Mesocycle & deloads
Run ~4–6 week accumulation blocks (RIR ~3 → ~0–1; in a SURPLUS/maintenance volume may creep toward
MAV — in a deficit the default is to hold sets flat, BUT the active 2026-06-20 override runs full
MAV for the current push block, see WORKOUT_PROTOCOL.md), then a **deload**
(~50% sets, moderate load) — every ~6 weeks or sooner if e1RM regresses across lifts while the
multi-day recovery trend is down. Don't deload off one red day. Track the meso week on each
workout sheet (recovery text), so the ramp and deload are executable, not vibes.

## Recovery science
Sleep is his strongest lever (slow-wave = physical, REM = cognitive; consistency matters). HRV
reflects parasympathetic tone; elevated RHR / skin-temp = early fatigue or illness. Judge trends,
not single readings.

## Getting lean without losing muscle (the cut)
- **Strength-retention index = e1RM trend vs bodyweight trend.** Strength holding while weight
  drops = muscle retained ✅. Strength falling + weight dropping = losing muscle → eat more / end cut.
  This is the **primary** muscle proxy (more reliable than the scale's noisy muscle reading).
- Rate of loss **0.5%/wk (~0.5–0.75 lb/wk)**, ceiling 1%/wk. Protein **~1.0–1.2 g/lb (~165 g/day)** —
  the #1 retention lever in a deficit. Keep training load heavy (heavy work says "keep this muscle").
- Cardio is a fine-tuning dial (steps/Zone-2), not the engine — food drives the deficit; hard
  intervals only at maintenance.
